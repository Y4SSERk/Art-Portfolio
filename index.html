The Artist

I am Yasser Khattach, a 22-year-old self-taught artist who has been drawing since childhood — not because someone taught me, but because creating felt like breathing.

Art, for me, is not decoration. It is the language I use when words collapse. Every line I draw carries an emotion too wild to be named, only shaped — described by visuals, lines, and the weight of graphite on paper.

"Limitations don't cage creativity — they force it to go deeper."

I work primarily in black and white. Pencil. No color. Because when you strip away color, you must master everything else — shape, shadow, contrast, texture. A red rose in pencil isn't obvious. It demands mastery of every detail until it speaks for itself.

The Philosophy

Constraint as Freedom

Choosing only black and white isn't a limitation — it's a liberation. Without color, every shadow, every edge, every grain of graphite becomes a decision. That's where real creativity lives.

Expression Over Imitation

I don't draw what the eye sees. I draw what the chest feels. Art is my translation of the interior world — the part that has no vocabulary in any spoken language.

Depth Through Darkness

Shadow defines form. In the absence of light is where the shape truly reveals itself. I am drawn to darkness not from sadness, but because that's where detail hides.

The Process

01

Feel First

Every piece starts with an emotion I can't put into words. I sit with it — sometimes for days — until it becomes a shape in my mind.

02

Sketch the Bones

Light, quick strokes. No commitment yet. Just letting the hand find the form that the feeling wants to take. Loose, exploratory, honest.

03

Build the Shadow

Darkness is where the detail lives. Layer by layer, I build shade and form until the subject begins to breathe on its own within the constraints of black and white.

04

Know When to Stop

Over-working kills a drawing. There's a moment when the piece tells you it's done. Learning to hear that moment is the hardest and most important skill.
